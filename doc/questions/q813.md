**The route.** A resolution given on a step is queued and taken when the step ends (`RN-R37`): `leaveStep ->
takePending -> takeAnswer`, whose routing enters the next round's ask, whose `asked` element's callback queues the next
resolution, which `takeAnswer`'s own `leaveStep` takes — nested, three frames a round. No scheduler is involved, so
`RN-R46`'s queue (W232, ADR 0146) does not touch it.

**Why it is not W232's.** W232 flattened what a SCHEDULER does inside `addTask`. This is what a CONSUMER does inside a
callback, on a different queue with its own throw semantics (`OR-R10` v0.19: a throw from a take reaches the caller,
and the attempts still pending behind it are appended by the step's own end) — restructuring `leaveStep` into one loop
moves those, and W232's first attempt is the example of a flattening that moved more than it meant to.

**Who meets it.** An automated approver answering every ask of a long loop from the same callback. A person answering
at rest does not: each answer is then its own step.

**Resolved by W233 (2026-11-15) — yes, one loop.** A step's end takes its pending calls and the stop in `takeCalls`;
a take's own end leaves the queue to it. A loop of 20,000 ask-only rounds answered on `asked` completes, round 2,400
asked at the depth round 1 was (`test/driver/settle-inside.test.ts`), and so does a chain of 20,000 pauses and
unpauses — the same recursion, which this question did not name and the conformity reader found. **The throw
semantics this question feared moving did not move**: 103,000 differential scenarios against the driver before the
change, 14 of them hand-built around a throwing take, agree element for element, report for report and throw for
throw. The order is stated in `spec/run-state.md` `RN-R46` v0.40. *(The overflow point depends on the arrangement:
about 3,500 rounds as first measured, 1,725 to 3,015 in W233's readers' runs.)*
